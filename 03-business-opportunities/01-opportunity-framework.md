# Business Opportunity Framework

## The Core Question

For every Claude capability, ask:
1. **Who is doing this manually today?**
2. **What does it cost them (time × hourly rate × volume)?**
3. **What would a 10x faster, 10x cheaper automated version be worth?**
4. **Why hasn't this been built yet?** (if the answer is "Claude didn't exist" — build it now)

## The Four Viable Business Models

### Model A: Vertical AI Assistant
**What:** An AI assistant purpose-built for a specific profession or industry.
**Example:** AI for patent attorneys, AI for radiologists, AI for financial auditors.
**Why it works:** General assistants (ChatGPT, Claude.ai) are 70% of what a professional needs. The last 30% — domain-specific tools, data sources, workflows — creates the moat.
**Revenue:** $50-500/user/month SaaS
**What Claude enables:** Domain-specific tool use, memory, structured outputs formatted for professional workflows

### Model B: Automated Pipeline Service
**What:** Replace a multi-step manual process with an autonomous pipeline.
**Example:** Document processing service, competitive intelligence service, content generation service.
**Why it works:** Businesses pay for outcomes, not AI access. The pipeline is the product.
**Revenue:** Per-unit pricing (per document, per report, per task) OR subscription
**What Claude enables:** Batch processing, tool use, managed agents running autonomously

### Model C: AI Integration Layer
**What:** Connect AI capabilities to existing enterprise systems where they have no AI.
**Example:** Add AI to their CRM, add AI to their ERP, add AI to their legacy software.
**Why it works:** Enterprises have existing workflows they can't abandon; they need AI layered on top.
**Revenue:** $10K-100K implementation + recurring hosting/maintenance
**What Claude enables:** Computer Use (no-API legacy systems), custom MCP servers, tool use against internal APIs

### Model D: Developer Infrastructure
**What:** Tools, libraries, or services that make it easier to build Claude-powered applications.
**Example:** MCP servers, prompt management, AI observability, evals framework.
**Why it works:** Developer tools market is lucrative; sells to technically sophisticated buyers who understand value.
**Revenue:** API/usage pricing, SaaS, or open source + support
**What Claude enables:** Building on top of the same API others are using

## How to Evaluate an Opportunity

Score each opportunity on these dimensions (1-5 each, max 25):

**1. Market Size** — How many potential buyers? How much is the manual process worth in aggregate?
**2. Pain Intensity** — How much does the problem hurt? Is it a "vitamin" (nice to have) or "painkiller" (must have)?
**3. Technical Feasibility** — Can this actually be built with current Claude capabilities? What are the edge cases?
**4. Competition** — Is this space empty, crowded, or dominated by a giant?
**5. Distribution Access** — Can you reach buyers? Do you have any unfair advantage?

**Score 18+:** Strong opportunity — prioritize
**Score 12-17:** Viable — explore further
**Score under 12:** Skip or wait

## The Rule of Specific Beats General

"AI for lawyers" is a worse opportunity than "AI for patent prosecution attorneys at firms with 10-50 attorneys."

The more specific your target:
- The more you can tailor the tools, prompts, and workflows
- The more you can charge (specialized = premium)
- The more your users self-identify and seek you out
- The easier word-of-mouth spreads

Start specific. Expand later if it works.
